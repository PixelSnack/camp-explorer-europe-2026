# PROPOSAL, not applied: reframed first-contact outreach mail

*Written 15 September 2026 after the Rafa Nadal Academy reply. Nothing in this file has been applied to the queued Gmail drafts. The current live template is `2026-09-06-wave2-data-request-and-base-fee-DRAFT.md` and it is unchanged.*

## What triggered this

Pedro Mota, Business Development Manager at Rafa Nadal Academy, replied on 15 September to the mail sent 14 September. His letter states that there is no signed agreement, that the collaboration was never presented to or authorised by their Commercial Department, that they were "somewhat surprised" by an administration fee and "in particular" by a potential invoice, and that until a formal agreement exists they "cannot accept any fee, commercial condition or payment obligation". He asks for documentation supporting "this alleged partnership".

He is right on the facts. No agreement exists. The listing is editorial, added by us in February 2026 from their public pages.

## The structural fault, not just the tone

The owner named three problems: not professional enough, arrogant, and the invoice does not belong in a first contact. There is a fourth underneath them, and it is the one that produced Pedro's letter.

**The mail never says that we added the camp ourselves, on our own initiative, and that the camp never asked for anything.** Without that sentence, every later sentence reads as though a commercial relationship already exists. The reader then encounters, in order: a service described as done "for you", a season granted "at no charge", a fee the listing "carries", and an invoice that "follows as soon as we have your details". For a recipient who has never heard of us, that is indistinguishable from a bill for an unordered service.

The specific lines that did the damage, in the order he met them:

1. "it is kept accurate for you" presumes we act on their behalf.
2. "your listing continues for the full 2026 season at no charge" implies a charge would otherwise be due, so something is being waived. Nothing was ever owed.
3. "every listing carries an annual administration fee of EUR 79" states the fee as a fact about them rather than an offer they may decline.
4. "we very much hope you will decide to pay it already now, as we're nearing the end of 2026" applies urgency to a stranger.
5. "Your invoice for the 2027 season follows as soon as we have your details" says an invoice is coming. Combined with the request for a VAT number and an invoicing address, this is an onboarding-to-billing flow the recipient never consented to. This is the line Pedro quoted back.
6. Nowhere is the recipient told they may decline, or that we will remove the listing on request.

The Auersperg version of 14 September is a real improvement in tone ("We hope you'll choose to stay with us", "we would appreciate your support as we keep developing the directory"), but it keeps all six faults above. Tone edits cannot fix it because the fault is the structure and the order.

## Two decisions only the owner can take

**Decision A. Split the mail in two.** First contact asks one question: would you like to stay listed for 2027. It names the price so there is no bait, but it does not ask for a VAT number, does not ask for an invoicing address, and does not say an invoice will follow. Those come in the second mail, after a yes. This is the owner's own point 3, and it also removes the unordered-invoice reading completely.

**Decision B. Reverse owner decision 3 of 7 September**, which reads: "the message projects a successful business (no apology, no mention of costs)". That decision is what produced the arrogance. The honest reason for the fee, that we carried the directory at our own cost for years and the seasonal re-verification has outgrown that, reads as candid and confident rather than weak, and it gives the reader a reason to say yes instead of an instruction to pay. Pedro's letter is the evidence. This is the owner's decision to reverse, not mine, which is why nothing has been applied.

## Rules for applying this, when the wording is settled

1. **Small batches only** (owner rule, 15 September). Rewrite in batches of four or five, re-read each draft from Gmail afterwards and diff it against the intended text, exactly as on 12 and 13 September.
2. **No internal comments or markers anywhere in a mail**, subject or body. Every bracketed placeholder in the template above must be resolved to real text or removed before a draft exists. A draft is never a place to leave a note.
3. **Replace, do not update** (existing rule): create the fresh draft, then retitle the old one so the owner can bin it. Owner clarification, 15 September: retitling a draft that is being thrown away is fine and is the intended use, because that mail is never sent. "DISCARD", "SUPERSEDED", "please delete" all serve. The no-internal-comments rule applies to mail that will actually go out, in its subject as much as its body. So a replacement is only ever titled with the real subject the operator should see, and only the dead copy carries a marker.
   A corollary: do not mark the 37 for deletion before their replacements exist. That would leave nothing to send and 37 to bin.
4. **The owner sends one first.** His proven sequence on 12 and 13 September was to send a single mail himself, edit it as he went, and then have his edits applied to the rest. The same order here: he takes one camp, sends it in his own words, and that sent version becomes the template for the other 36.
5. Sender: API drafts default to the personal address. The owner picks partnerships@ in the From dropdown each time, and closes the space in the web address, keeping the dot.
